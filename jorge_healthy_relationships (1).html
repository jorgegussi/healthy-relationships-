WHAT IS AN INTIMATE RELATIONSHIP?

A relationship is when two people are close to each other. This can be a friend, a partner, or family. There are two kinds of relationships: healthy and unhealthy. It is very important to know the difference!

"Healthy relationships make you feel safe, happy, and respected."

In this project, I will explain what makes a relationship healthy or unhealthy. I will also look at respect, real examples, and how the media changes what we think about love and friendship.

    WHY DOES RESPECT SHOW UP EVERYWHERE?

    Respect means you value the other person. You listen to them. You do not hurt them. Without respect, a relationship cannot be healthy. Respect is the base of everything.

    Respect also means you respect yourself. If you do not respect yourself, it is hard to have a healthy relationship.

    3 KEY CONCEPTS FOR A HEALTHY RELATIONSHIP

    These three ideas help people build strong, healthy relationships:

    💡 INSIGHT Knowing your own feelings and why you act the way you do.
    🔄 MUTUALITY Both people care about each other equally. It goes both ways.
    😌 EMOTION REGULATION Staying calm and not hurting others when you feel angry or sad.
    MEDIA ANALYSIS — CLAIRE & BENDER (THE BREAKFAST CLUB)

    Claire is popular and rich. Bender is rebellious and angry. They are very different. In the movie, Bender is sometimes rude to Claire and crosses her boundaries.

    This relationship is mostly unhealthy at the start — but they learn to understand each other.

    Bender does not always show respect. He says hurtful things. Claire feels uncomfortable. This is an unhealthy sign.

    To improve, Bender needs to work on emotion regulation. He gets very angry fast. Claire also needs insight — to understand why she stays close to someone who disrespects her.

    LGBTQ+ RELATIONSHIPS — LOVE IS LOVE

    Gay, lesbian, bisexual, and transgender people have relationships too. All the same healthy relationship rules apply. Every person deserves love, respect, and safety — no matter who they love.

    "A healthy relationship is healthy because of how people treat each other — not because of who they are."

    In Canada, same-sex marriage has been legal since 2005. LGBTQ+ people have the right to be in relationships just like everyone else.

    ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~

    What makes an LGBTQ+ relationship healthy?

    🏳️‍🌈 ACCEPTANCE Both people accept who the other person is. They do not try to change them.
    🗣️ OPEN COMMUNICATION Talking honestly about identity, feelings, and needs is very important.
    🛡️ SAFETY Feeling safe to be yourself is a key part of any healthy relationship.
    🤲 SUPPORT LGBTQ+ people may face challenges outside the relationship. A good partner supports them through this.
    - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

    Myths vs. Facts

    ✗ MYTH
    Gay relationships are not as real or as serious as straight relationships.
    ✓ FACT
    All relationships can be deep, loving, and serious. Who you love does not change that.
    ✗ MYTH
    LGBTQ+ relationships do not have problems like other couples.
    ✓ FACT
    All couples face challenges. LGBTQ+ couples also need good communication and respect.
    ✗ MYTH
    Domestic abuse does not happen in same-sex relationships.
    ✓ FACT
    Abuse can happen in any relationship. LGBTQ+ people can also be victims and need support.
    ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~

    Extra challenges LGBTQ+ people may face:

    • Some families do not accept their relationship at first
    • They may face discrimination from society
    • They may have less support around them
    • This can put more pressure on the relationship

    This is why respect, communication, and support are even more important in these relationships. A healthy LGBTQ+ relationship helps both people feel proud of who they are.

    MY PERSONAL REFLECTION

    Does the media change how we see relationships?

    Yes! Movies and TV shows make us think certain things are normal, even when they are not. For example, many movies show jealousy as love. But jealousy is actually unhealthy. Also, the media is getting better at showing LGBTQ+ relationships in a positive way, which helps people feel accepted.

    ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~

    What has the biggest impact on my beliefs about relationships?

    My family has the biggest impact. They show me how to treat people every day. My culture and friends also shape what I think is normal in a relationship.

    ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~

    Are friendship and romantic relationships different for me?

    Yes. In a friendship, I expect honesty and fun. In a romantic relationship, I also expect deeper trust and more communication. But respect is important in both — and this is true for ALL relationships, including LGBTQ+ ones!
